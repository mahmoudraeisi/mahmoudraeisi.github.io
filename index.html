Where should signal processing happen in a radio?

I am a signal processing researcher working on the physical layer of wireless communication and sensing. Rather than leaving all computation to the digital baseband, I design metasurfaces, reconfigurable antennas and the propagation environment to take part in beamforming, estimation and inference in the wave domain, to reduce the hardware cost, energy and latency of 6G transceivers.

Research

Computing with the wave

My work builds on estimation and detection theory, array signal processing and optimization, and increasingly on machine learning.

With two Ph.D. students and collaborators in Sweden, Finland, Greece and the USA, I am building a research line on wireless physical neural networks for task-oriented sensing.

  • Physical neural networks and analog computing

    Wireless physical neural networks in which radio hardware, such as stacked intelligent metasurfaces (SIMs), pinching antennas and relays, acts together with the channel as the layers of a neural network. Part of the inference is then done by analog computing on the propagating wave.

  • Task-oriented sensing

    Wireless sensing designed for the end task rather than for full signal or channel recovery, using standard communication signals and reconfigurable hardware.

    • human posture recognition
    • vehicle tracking
    • object counting
    • area occupancy detection

  • Novel transceiver architectures

    Design and analysis of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), beyond-diagonal RIS (BD-RIS), SIMs, flexible intelligent layered metasurfaces (FILM) and pinching-antenna systems (PASS) that move signal processing into the analog and wave domain.

    OutputsJ1J3J4C1S1

  • Estimation, detection and integrated sensing and communication

    Localization, Cramér–Rao-bound-driven ISAC beamforming, sensing-mode management for multi-cell ISAC, and index and spatial modulation with low-complexity detection for mmWave and massive MIMO.

    OutputsJ2J6J8C1C2C3S1S2

  • Sim2Real for localization and sensing Initial investigation

    How models trained on simulated, ray-traced data can be transferred to real measurements. An independent research line that can also serve as the next step for physical neural networks. I coordinate the CROSSNET Sim2Real working group.
